Abstract

Diffusion-based navigation policies can generate diverse short-horizon trajectory hypotheses from visual observations, enabling effective exploration under uncertainty[cite: 3]. However, when deployed in grass-dominant outdoor environments, these generative policies often lack explicit spatial safety regulation, leading to boundary violations under visually ambiguous grass-non-grass transitions[cite: 4].

We propose Spatially Verified Diffusion (SVD), a modular framework that augments diffusion-based trajectory generation with an external spatial verification layer[cite: 5]. Instead of modifying or retraining the diffusion model, SVD constructs a transient robot-centric bird's-eye-view (BEV) feasibility space from semantic traversability prediction and camera geometry, and filters candidate trajectories before execution[cite: 6].

Extensive real-world experiments across flat and uneven terrains demonstrate that SVD significantly reduces boundary violations and increases minimum clearance compared with an unconstrained diffusion baseline, while maintaining real-time performance (2.8 Hz) on embedded hardware[cite: 11].

Methodology

At each control step, the robot receives an RGB observation[cite: 110]. A diffusion-based navigation policy generates N candidate short-horizon trajectories conditioned on recent observations[cite: 111]. SVD constructs a local feasibility representation from semantic traversability perception and rejects candidate trajectories that violate explicit spatial constraints in BEV space[cite: 113].

The overall pipeline is implemented in a ROS 2 closed loop to support real robot deployment and profiling[cite: 108]. A YOLO-style segmentation network trained on a self-collected dataset (4k+ labeled images) balances accuracy and inference latency, which is critical for real-time navigation loops on embedded hardware[cite: 128].
